What can I do?

      
    
        
  • Don't burn to clear land or rubbish in the dry season, especially on peat: peat fires smoulder
          underground for weeks and are very hard to put out.
    • 
        
  • Report a fire early to your local fire brigade or disaster agency. Small fires are the easy ones to stop.
    • 
        
  • On smoky days, stay indoors with windows closed, wear a well-fitted N95/KN95-type mask outside,
          and look after children, older people and anyone with asthma or heart disease.
    • 
        
  • Follow official warnings from your national weather, air-quality and disaster services.
    • 
        
  • Not every fire is a disaster. In Northern Australia, rangers burn early in the dry season on
          purpose, so later fires are smaller.

Read before use

        
    
          
  • Not a live alert system. Monthly totals, updated when the pipeline is rebuilt
            (latest complete month: n/a). For fires burning now, use the
            NASA FIRMS Fire Map or FIRMS area alerts.
    • 
          
  • Hotspots are not only wildfires. They include agricultural, land-clearing, peat and planned savanna
            burns. A high month can mean more planned burning, not a disaster.
    • 
          
  • Hotspots are not fires or burned area. One large fire gives many hotspots; cloud and smoke can hide fires.
            Counts are estimates in NOAA-20 units; see the validation error under Method.
