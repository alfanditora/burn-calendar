01 · The problem

One planet, three counts

Every dry season, fires burn across Indonesia. In 2015 alone, satellites recorded 812,705 fire hotspots, the year of the Southeast Asian haze crisis. People who plan for fire season need one simple answer: is this season worse than normal, and where?

NASA has watched these fires for 25 years through its FIRMS service. But the record comes from three different satellite sensors, and they do not count fires the same way.

Who watched, and when

Different eyes

MODIS sees 1 km pixels; VIIRS sees 375 m. Over the same Indonesian land, VIIRS reports about 5.1 times more hotspots than MODIS.

A handover now

Suomi NPP fire production stopped on Nov 2, 2026. MODIS on Terra and Aqua is drifting from its orbit and retiring.

The result

A raw count from 2005 (MODIS) and one from 2025 (NOAA-20) are not comparable: a naive series shows a fake jump when VIIRS arrives.

We express everything in NOAA-20-equivalent hotspots per month, because NOAA-20 is the sensor that continues. Each link is calibrated on the years when both sensors were flying.

1

Download. The FIRMS area API, in 5-day chunks, for MODIS, VIIRS Suomi NPP and VIIRS NOAA-20. Standard (archive) data for history; near-real-time data only after the archive ends.

2

Clean. Keep confident detections (MODIS confidence ≥ 30, VIIRS nominal or high). Remove volcanoes, gas flares and offshore sources using the FIRMS type flags.

3

Count. Inside real country and province borders (Natural Earth), per month and per 25 km map square.

4

Calibrate. Ratio of counts on overlap years for each link, with a drift check: MODIS orbits changed in recent years, so early and late overlap years are compared.

5

Validate. Hold out each overlap year in turn, predict it from the others, and weight the errors by fire counts.

6

Analyse. Mann-Kendall trend test and Sen’s slope; anomaly against the median of the same month in earlier years; fire season; early-signal hindcast.

Rules we kept

  • Never invent data: every number comes from FIRMS
  • Archive data first; near-real-time only where the archive ends
  • Every threshold lives in one settings file and the method notes
  • One command rebuilds everything; 32 automated tests pass

Why real borders matter. With a rectangle around Riau, October 2026 looked “well above normal” (+86%), because the box also caught fires in neighbouring provinces. Inside Riau’s real border the same month is “below normal” (−88%).

03 · The app for everyone

Explore & Play

The home screen: pick a mode; the latest month in every area is one tap away.

A pixel time machine. Press play and watch 25 years of fire seasons light up, month by month, with notes at well-known events such as the 2015 haze crisis.

Routine or unusual? A hotspot is not always a wildfire: farm, land-clearing, peat and planned burns look the same from space. So the map shows where burning is routine for that place and month (grey, 50–150% of usual) and where it is far above normal (red, 2× usual or more), which is more likely an uncontrolled fire.

Games and badges. Four quick games on the real numbers (guess the fire season, is this month normal?, higher or lower, find the worst year), XP and badges, and the story of the satellite handover.

What can I do? Plain advice for smoky days and for reporting fires early.

04 · The app for responders and researchers

Analyst View & any area

The burning-activity calendar, 2001–2026, with the anomaly badge and the trend card.

Analyst View

  • Calendar of every month, with a date filter and CSV download
  • Anomaly badge: this month against the median of the same month in earlier years
  • Trend test (Mann-Kendall, Sen’s slope) in plain words
  • Season tracker: this year so far against the usual, lowest and highest years
  • Early signal: does an above-normal run-up predict the peak?
  • Most unusual places: the 25 km squares furthest above their own normal
  • Situation brief to copy or print, built from the numbers
  • Open files: CSV and JSON for every area, the method and validation

Any area

Four featured areas get the full analysis. Any of 33 Indonesian provinces and 3 Australian states can be opened from the list or by tapping the map, and you can draw your own box to get its monthly record, its usual month and a CSV. Every view has a shareable link.

Early signal. The two months before the busiest months (the “run-up”) tell us something: in every area, run-up and peak are clearly correlated. A quiet run-up is the more reliable message; an above-normal run-up raises the chance of an above-normal peak to about 2–3 times the base rate, but it misses too. It is a historical pattern, not a forecast.

06 · Impact and next steps

From hotspots to decisions

Who it helps

  • Disaster agencies (such as BNPB and provincial BPBD): is this season worse than normal, and where?
  • Peatland and plantation managers: when the risky months start
  • Researchers: one consistent 25-year record with open files
  • The public: explore, play, and know what to do on smoky days

Next steps

  • Early warnings for disaster agencies
  • Full analysis for every province, with current borders
  • Peat and land-cover maps to tell farm burning from forest and peat fires
  • NOAA-21 and automatic monthly updates, paired with FIRMS email alerts
  • An AI plain-language brief, every number checked against the data

Honest limits

  • Hotspots are not burned area; cloud and smoke hide some fires
  • Not only wildfires: farm, clearing, peat and planned burns too
  • Monthly, not a live alert system (live fires: the NASA FIRMS Fire Map)
  • Converted counts are estimates; the error is shown for each area

Data and references

NASA FIRMS active fire data: MODIS C6.1 (Terra, Aqua), VIIRS Suomi NPP and VIIRS NOAA-20, standard and near-real-time, via the FIRMS area API. Natural Earth borders (public domain). pandas, NumPy, pyarrow, shapely, pymannkendall (Hussain & Mahmud, 2019), requests, pytest. Fonts: Press Start 2P, VT323 (SIL OFL). Full list: README.
